# The math functions

## Code generation

The constants and tables of `da_sincos.c` and `da_log.c` are generated
by two C programs, `gen_sincos.c` and `gen_log.c`. They are not part
of the library and need MPFR and GMP. `make gen_sincos` and `make
gen_log` build them. Each prints on stdout the block to paste over the
generated block of its file, from the line `Generated by` to the end
of the last table, and some information on stderr.

What the two programs share, the working precision, the rounding to
fixed point and the fits of polynomials, is in `gen_approx.c`.

Every value is computed in MPFR at 256 bits, then converted to the
fixed point of its table, rounded to nearest with ties to even, but
for the three kinds of constants at the end of this section. The
values of the tables are correctly rounded results of MPFR, or
products of two, and need little more than the 64 bits kept; the
fitted polynomials need about 40 bits more, the top coefficients of a
polynomial on so short an interval multiplying powers as small as
5e-13, and change below 128 bits of precision. 256 bits is twice
that. The functions MPFR provides, π, sine, cosine, powers of ten,
logarithms and `atanh`, are used directly; only the coefficients of
the polynomials are fitted, that is, solved for so that the
polynomial approximates its function as closely as possible over its
interval. Remez's exchange does it, minimizing the largest weighted
error on a grid of 2000 points, each step solving its linear system
on exact GMP rationals, so that its own rounding never enters the
fit:

  - `(1 - sin θ/θ) / θ²` and `(1 - cos θ) / θ²`, the polynomials of
    `da_sincos.c`, for the absolute error of θ² times the polynomial,
    for θ up to 4.5036 degrees, the step of the table.

  - `(10^d - 1) / d`, the polynomial of `da_exp10`, for the error
    relative to 10^d, over [0, 1/16].

  - `2 / ln 10 atanh(z) / z`, the polynomial in z² of `da_log10`, for
    its relative error. The bound on z is not a constant: the program
    computes it from the ends of the zones.

The kernels truncate their products, so the error of a result before
its rounding is not centred on zero, and a constant moved by a
fraction of its unit centres it where it counts most, in the lowest
decade of a result. `bias`, in `gen_approx.c`, adds that fraction
before the rounding. The fractions are measured: a copy of the
library records what `nearest` receives, MPFR gives its error, and
every representation of the range where this error peaks is then
tried, those whose result is next to a midpoint being checked.

  - The sines of the table of `da_sincos.c` are 0.44 unit of Q64
    above their values. The step of the table is 2^52 femtodegrees,
    4.5036 degrees, so that the index is a shift, and its points
    are the multiples of the step less an offset, which is chosen
    for the sines: of the 100000 least offsets that keep 4.0032
    degrees in the first slice, it is the one for which the five
    sines below 0.4, so raised, are the closest to Q64 integers,
    within 0.05 unit. The largest error found for `da_sin` and
    `da_cos` went from 0.5084 unit in the last place to 0.5040.

  - `F17`, the 10^-17 of `da_exp10`, is 0.96 unit of Q120 above its
    value, the nearest there is to the 0.6 that cancels the
    truncation of its product when 10^x is about to leave its lowest
    decade. The largest error found went from 0.5326 to 0.5302; what
    remains is mostly the error of the polynomial.

  - The constant term of `ATANH` is 2 units of Q64 above its fit,
    for the arguments of `da_log10` next to 1, where the truncations
    all make the result smaller. The largest error found went from
    0.5137 to 0.5100, now in a zone away from 1, where it is that of
    the table `T`, in Q63 and rounded to nearest.

The tangent has no constant of its own. Its largest error found,
0.5181, is that of the inverse of a small tangent, where the
truncations of the divisor all go the same way.

The arguments of the largest errors found are in the next section.

## Worst cases

The functions of `da_sincos.c` and `da_log.c` are not correctly
rounded: each documents a bound on its error, in units in the last
place of the exact result, as `test-math-random.c` defines and
measures it. This section records the largest errors known, as of
2026-11-13, with the constants that the generators print on that
date. They are the largest found, not proven maxima, but each is
close to what the error can be at all, computed below: within
0.00002 for the sine, 0.00004 for the cosine, 0.0004 for the
tangent, 0.0002 for ten to the x and 0.0001 for the logarithm.

### The bounds

The bounds are computed, not measured: half a unit for the rounding,
plus the most that the error before the rounding can be, rounded up
to the third decimal. For each argument, the constants are taken as
they are, the polynomials with the error they have there, and every
truncation of the kernel as anything from nothing to a unit of its
place, whatever the others are, which is more freedom than the
arguments have. The largest sum of these over the arguments of each
function:

| function   | before rounding | where                                |
|------------|-----------------|--------------------------------------|
| `da_sin`   | 0.00398         | 8.506799, a point of the table       |
| `da_cos`   | 0.00398         | 81.493201, the same sine             |
| `da_tan`   | 0.01845         | 88.569048, the result below 40.032   |
| `da_exp10` | 0.03030         | fraction of x of 0.5988, x below 0   |
| `da_log10` | 0.01003         | 1.7960, in the zone of the largest   |

The computation was checked on 2.4 million arguments of each
function, the sine and the cosine sharing theirs: in exact
arithmetic, it gives the value that the library hands to its
rounding, and the error of that value is between the two ends
computed for its argument, within 0.0001 of one of them for some.

### Where they are

The error before the rounding counts most in the lowest decade of a
result, where a unit of the fixed point is worth the most units in
the last place, and is largest at the top of that decade when it is
relative.

  - Sine and cosine: a reduced angle next to a point of the table,
    here 8.5068 degrees, where the sine is below 0.4. The error
    before rounding is within 0.0040 on both sides of every such
    point.

  - Tangent: the inverse of the tangent of a small angle, when the
    result is just below 40.032, the top of a decade. The 60 bits
    kept of the divisor and two other truncations all make the
    result larger, by up to 0.018. The same happens below 89.857
    and 89.9857 degrees.

  - Ten to the x: a fraction of x of 0.599, the last extremum of
    the error of the polynomial before 10^x reaches 4.0032 and
    leaves its lowest decade.

  - Logarithm: an argument away from 1 whose logarithm is below 0.4
    in magnitude, in a zone whose entry of the table `T`, a Q63
    rounded to nearest, is among the furthest from its value.

### How they were found

The 64-bit haystack is too large for random testing to get close: one
million random inputs for each function reach only the following bounds:
```
sin     1000000 checks, bound 0.504, worst 0.50225 at 0x40457c5c701780fa
cos     1000000 checks, bound 0.504, worst 0.50251 at 0x401a2bd81010df35
tan     1000000 checks, bound 0.519, worst 0.50777 at 0xc03e3f3dbf4b107e
exp10   1000000 checks, bound 0.531, worst 0.52382 at 0xbfc1ac17cb8a82ec
log10   1000000 checks, bound 0.511, worst 0.50685 at 0x3f1fb6b33e9bb2a5
```

Instead, an instrumented version of the library records the 128-bit
value that `nearest` receives, and MPFR gives its error on a regular
grid of arguments: this shows where the error before rounding peaks.
Every consecutive representation of such a place is then tried, a few
billions of them, and those whose value before rounding is next to a
midpoint are checked against MPFR. The result is wrong by half a unit
plus the distance of the exact value to the midpoint when the two are
on different sides of it.

The constants of `da_sin`, `da_exp10` and `da_log10` were then centred
on their truncations, as the end of the previous section tells.
Before that, the largest errors found were 0.50844, 0.50842, 0.51807,
0.53262 and 0.51374, at the five arguments that end the table of
`test-fixed.c`.
